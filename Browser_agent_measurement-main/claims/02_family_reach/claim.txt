CLAIM 2 - Agents reach kinds of tracking services that the people in the
study almost never touch on the same tasks.

Paper
    Abstract ("cross-device TV-correlation enrichers (0% human vs. 9.3%
    agent) and native recommendation engines (1.4% vs. 9.3%)"),
    Section 7.3, and Table 4.

Run
    bash claims/02_family_reach/run.sh

    Under a second. Same 220 human and 140 agent sessions as claim 1.

What you should see
    Share of sessions contacting at least one host in each family, with the
    artifact's one-sided Fisher test for agent greater than human.

        family                        human %   agent %    Fisher p
        cross-device ACR                  0.0       9.3     3.3e-06
        native recommendation             1.4       9.3     5.3e-04
        long-tail (other)                74.5      92.1     1.2e-05
        identity resolution               9.5      10.7        0.42
        audience measurement              9.5      10.7        0.42
        open-RTB exchange                28.6      26.4        0.72
        platform first-party ad-tech     55.0      39.3        1.00

How this compares to the paper
    All seven rows match Table 4 on all four printed columns, mean tracker
    hosts and touch percentage for each cohort. The abstract's 0% vs 9.3%
    and 1.4% vs 9.3% reproduce as 0.000 vs 9.286 and 1.364 vs 9.286.

    Two families run the other way, and the paper reports them that way.
    Platform first-party ad tags are touched by 55.0% of human sessions
    against 39.3% of agent sessions, and open-RTB contact is comparable at
    28.6% against 26.4%. The claim is about which families agents reach,
    not a uniform increase.

    The Fisher p column is an artifact addition. For this table the paper
    reports a binomial-GLM likelihood-ratio test, which this run does not
    perform. The run also prints a false_positive row with blank odds ratio
    and p. It is classifier bookkeeping, not one of Table 4's families.
